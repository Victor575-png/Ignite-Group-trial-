How to Choose the Right Graphic Designer for Your Brand

By the Ignite Group Team · Graphic Design · 5 min read

Hiring a designer feels straightforward until you're staring at ten different portfolios and can't tell who's actually right for your brand. Here's how to narrow it down without guessing.

Look past "pretty" — look for range and reasoning

A strong portfolio doesn't just show attractive designs. It shows a designer who can adapt style to a brief — a church poster looks different from a corporate logo, which looks different from a product flyer. If every piece in a portfolio looks the same, that designer may only have one style, not the flexibility your brand needs over time.

Questions worth asking before you hire

  • "Can you walk me through why you made this choice?" — A good designer can explain their decisions (color, layout, typography), not just present a nice-looking result.
  • "What do you need from me to get started?" — This tells you if they'll ask for a proper brief (goals, audience, brand colors) or just start guessing.
  • "How many revisions are included?" — Get this in writing before you start, so it doesn't become a disagreement later.
  • "What file formats will I receive?" — You want editable source files (like .ai, .psd, or Canva links), not just a flattened image you can't adjust later.

Red flags to watch for

  • No questions asked before quoting a price — a rushed quote usually means a rushed design.
  • A portfolio full of other brands' well-known logos or templates with just the colors changed.
  • No willingness to explain their process or timeline.
  • Consistently missed deadlines during the hiring conversation itself — a preview of what's to come.

What fair pricing looks like

Pricing in Kenya varies widely by experience and scope, but as a rule: the cheapest option is rarely the best value once you factor in revisions, missed deadlines, and files you can't reuse. A mid-range designer who delivers on brief the first time, with clean source files, usually costs less in the long run than three rounds of "cheap" redesigns.

Why full-service beats a one-off freelancer

A single designer can make you one great poster. A team that also handles your social media and content strategy makes sure that poster actually looks consistent with everything else your brand puts out — same colors, same fonts, same tone — because it was all planned together.
